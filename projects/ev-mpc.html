EV powertrain: MPC speed controller

In a team project on the longitudinal speed control of an electric vehicle, modelled in Simulink, I designed the state-space model predictive speed controller. This page shows only that part.

Role
Team project; my part was the MPC speed controller
Vehicle model
1.55 MW, four motors, modelled as one equivalent PMSM (shared team model)
Controller
Augmented state-space MPC on the force increment
Implementation
MATLAB Function block with a precomputed receding-horizon gain
When
2026
Controller sample time, with a zero-order hold
0.10 s
Drive modes (Eco, Comfort, Sport), each with its own linearisation speed, weights and force limits for the MPC
3

Results, as reported

  • Lag on the ramp. In Comfort mode the speed fell about 11.5 km/h behind on the steep high-speed ramp and recovered once the ramp flattened: a dynamic lag from the linearised drag model and the finite horizon, not a static offset.
  • Final hold. At the 250 km/h hold the MPC kept a residual speed error of about 0.26, 0.17 and 0.06 km/h in Eco, Comfort and Sport.
  • Road-slope step. On a 0 to 5 % slope step at a constant 150 km/h in Comfort mode the MPC dipped about 1.5 km/h and returned to the set point with zero steady speed error.

What I took from it

  1. A linear model has a price. With a linearised drag model and a finite horizon, the controller lags on sustained accelerations, which an integrating PI loop avoids.
  2. Precompute what you can. Because the formulation is linear, the receding-horizon gain is computed offline and the block only evaluates a light law at each sample.
  3. Limits belong inside the optimisation. The controller saturates the output of an unconstrained law; the next step identified was an online QP solver that enforces the force limits itself.
  4. What the controller knows matters. The Simulink block assumes a constant reference over the horizon, which makes its tracking slightly more conservative than a MATLAB loop that knows the future steps.

Scope

Team project. The vehicle model, the field-oriented current control, the adaptive cruise control and the report were shared work and are not presented here as mine. The results above are as given in the team's submitted report.
